## Introduction

Recent years have seen increasing interest in multimodal monitoring. Previous studies have shown that single-channel approaches work under limited conditions. However, the mechanisms underlying cross-modal fusion remain poorly understood. Few studies have examined temporal alignment at scale. Here, we investigate whether a unified encoder improves robustness. This work provides a controlled comparison against strong baselines.

## Methods

A cross-sectional study was undertaken to evaluate the encoder. Samples were collected from three public cohorts. X was quantified using a standard pipeline under standard conditions. Differences were analysed statistically. All analyses were performed in Python 3.11.

## Results

Figure 1 shows the main comparison across cohorts. The proposed model achieves higher AUROC than baselines. Accuracy increases from 0.71 to 0.84 on the held-out set. We find that latency is significantly lower for the compact variant. Contrary to expectations, calibration error remains stable.

## Discussion

These results prove that multimodal fusion is necessary in all settings. The method clearly demonstrates superiority and is unprecedented in this domain. A possible explanation is improved temporal alignment. Previous work reported mixed findings, and this discrepancy may reflect cohort shift. These results should be interpreted with caution because external validation was limited. Further work is needed to determine whether the gains generalise.

## Conclusion

This study set out to test a unified multimodal encoder. The present findings indicate a consistent accuracy gain on held-out data. These results extend our understanding of cross-modal alignment. Notwithstanding these limitations, the approach may support further clinical investigation.
